# When the Head Is Bruised and Under Attack
**By Dr. Anthony Onoja**

![When the Head Is Bruised](https://donmaston09.github.io/CraftChronicles/assets/burden_of_the_crown.png)

“And the soldiers plaited a crown of thorns, and put it on His head, and they put on Him a purple robe,” — John 19:2

Do not read this casually. Read it with understanding.

There is a reason the head is always under attack.

We are in the season of Easter, a time when Christians across the world reflect on the death and resurrection of Jesus Christ. Yet beyond the celebration lies a pattern many have failed to recognise.

Recently, I listened to a woman’s story. She met her husband when he had very little. They built their lives together from a place of humility. Over time, through diligence and grace, he prospered. He moved his family into a better environment, an estate.

Then something changed.

She began to associate with other women who influenced her perception. They encouraged her to place pressure on her husband, to demand more, to measure his worth by material display. Gradually, respect turned into contempt. Honour became criticism. The man she once supported became the man she diminished.

Conflict followed.

He left the house, had an accident, fell into a coma, and died.

And the voices that once advised her vanished, leaving her alone to bear the weight of consequences she did not fully understand when she acted.

This is not merely a story. It is a pattern.

### Why the Head Is Targeted

The man, the head, is not simply a participant in life. He is a target.

To understand this, you must return to the beginning.

In Genesis, after the fall of man, God declared that the seed of the woman would bruise the serpent’s head, while the serpent would bruise his heel. This was not poetic language. It was a revelation of strategy.

The head represents authority, direction, and glory.

The heel represents movement.

When conflict arises, the enemy does not aim for movement. He aims for control.

He aims for the head.

### The Meaning of the Crown of Thorns

The crown of thorns placed upon the head of Christ was not incidental. It was intentional.

It was not merely an instrument of pain. It was a message.

No crown comes without suffering.  
No authority is established without sacrifice.  
No glory is revealed without endurance.  

Many desire elevation, yet reject the process that produces it. They seek recognition without refinement, honour without discipline, and influence without responsibility.

But the pattern is clear.

Christ wore thorns before He wore glory.

Pain preceded position.

And today, He is seated in honour beyond human comprehension.

### When the Head Suffers, the Body Follows

The head is not an isolated structure. It is the command centre of the body.

It carries vision.  
It processes reality.  
It determines direction.  

When the head is damaged, the body does not negotiate. It suffers.

This is why the attack is relentless.

If the enemy cannot destroy the head outright, he will exhaust it. He will burden it. He will surround it with pressure until clarity becomes difficult and strength begins to fade.

Look at the modern man.

He is blamed by systems.  
Drained by labour.  
Expected by family.  
Judged by society.  

The pressure is constant.

The heel may move, but the head carries the weight.

And if the head collapses, everything beneath it follows.

### A Reflection on Nations

This principle extends beyond the individual.

Consider nations.

A country may possess resources, intelligence, and potential, yet remain stagnant if its leadership is compromised. When the head of a nation is misaligned, the body suffers consequences it did not directly create.

Decisions become irrational.  
Priorities become distorted.  
Progress becomes delayed.  

The enemy does not need to destroy the people if he can destabilise the head.

### The Responsibility of Leadership

Leadership is not comfort. It is sacrifice.

The crown of thorns reveals a truth many avoid: the head must endure what the body cannot.

True leadership absorbs pressure so that others may function.

Yet today, many reverse this order.

They protect themselves and transfer the burden to those they lead.

This is not leadership. It is exploitation.

Leadership must cost you.

If you are unwilling to carry weight, you are not prepared to lead.

### The Discipline of Thought

The head is not only symbolic. It is functional.

It must think clearly.  
It must act strategically.  
It must consider the future beyond the present moment.  

When leaders make decisions that favour personal comfort over collective good, they betray their role.

Leadership requires discipline, not impulse.

Vision, not convenience.

### Alignment Between Head and Body

The head cannot function without the body.

And the body cannot thrive without the head.

There must be alignment.

In many societies, this alignment is broken. Leaders and followers operate as though they are adversaries. Suspicion replaces trust. Division replaces cooperation.

No system can rise under such conditions.

Progress requires unity.

Within the family, this principle is equally true.

The head must protect and provide.  
The body must support and strengthen.  

It is not a one-sided responsibility. It is a structure.

When properly aligned, it produces stability, growth, and legacy.

### The Verdict

The head is bruised because it carries weight.

It is attacked because it holds authority.

It is pressured because it determines direction.

This is not accidental. It is strategic.

So do not be surprised by the pressure.

Understand it.

Prepare for it.

Endure it.

Because the crown you seek will not come without the thorns you must first carry.

### Final Charge

In this season, gain wisdom.

Discern the patterns around you.  
Support what carries responsibility.  
Strengthen what holds structure.  

And if you are called to lead, do not shrink from the cost.

Carry it with clarity.  
Endure it with discipline.  
Fulfil it with purpose.  

For when the head stands firm, the body survives.

**Shalom.**
